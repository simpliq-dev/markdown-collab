# Write public copy

Make public prose sound like a thoughtful person with a real point of view, without requiring the human to design a style system before work can begin.

## Infer before asking

Inspect the artifact, project, surrounding copy, product evidence, and conversation. Infer a provisional brief covering:

- audience and likely knowledge;
- channel and reader task;
- purpose, desired action, and likely objections;
- author or brand relationship to the reader;
- suitable register, directness, rhythm, and detail; and
- factual claims, constraints, and uncertainties.

When available, inspect two to five representative passages from the same author, brand, or product. Infer recurring choices without mechanically imitating incidental mannerisms.

Do not turn this into a questionnaire. Make reasonable assumptions from evidence and state only assumptions that materially affect the result. If evidence is thin, propose a plausible style instead of asking the human to define one from scratch.

## Calibrate before rewriting

- For short reversible work or a clearly established voice, proceed and briefly name the style used.
- For extensive, consequential, or materially ambiguous public copy, first offer a compact style proposal and representative sample, such as an opening paragraph or two distinct directions. Explain the tradeoff plainly and invite correction before applying the full rewrite.
- If the human explicitly asks for a direct rewrite, proceed unless a genuine ambiguity would materially change the outcome.
- Treat approval of wording separately from committing, publishing, or sending it. Do not perform those actions unless requested.

## Write from evidence

1. Preserve intent, useful technical detail, and honest uncertainty. Do not invent customer outcomes, maturity, compatibility, authority, or social proof.
2. Separate the author's stated views from editorial choices. Do not turn a factual premise into praise, preference, confidence, or sentiment the author did not express.
3. Prefer language that names concrete actors, actions, objects, and consequences. Use a problem-led opening when it helps the reader, not as a mandatory formula.
4. Keep the author's texture through real vocabulary, constraints, tradeoffs, and small details. Prefer specific meaning over polished language that could describe any product.
5. Flag clichés, hype, business jargon, and familiar promotional formulas when they obscure meaning. Treat words such as "seamlessly", "unlock", and "robust" as prompts to check specificity, not automatic deletions.
6. Match structure and length to the channel and reader task. Use headings, examples, summaries, or calls to action only when they improve comprehension.
7. Use intimacy, enthusiasm, humour, and rhetorical questions only when they fit the author, audience, and purpose.

## Review the result

Compare the draft with the provisional brief, source material, and voice examples. Check that:

- the register fits this author or brand, this reader, and their relationship;
- meaning and factual claims survived the rewrite;
- limitations are visible where they affect a reasonable decision;
- the structure suits the medium;
- repeated phrasing does not make the copy feel templated; and
- the reader can tell what matters and what to do next.

Use the "could this describe ten unrelated products?" test as a diagnostic for interchangeable copy, not a universal rule. For consequential or repeatedly used copy, compare one or two alternatives against the brief instead of relying on first-pass preference.

## Medium-specific defaults

- For release notes, state what changed and why it matters without turning routine maintenance into a launch story.
- For READMEs, make the relevant subset of purpose, fit, installation, common use, trust boundaries, and current limitations easy to find.
- For UI copy, prioritize the user's immediate task, available space, and action consequence over brand flourish.

## Output

For a rewrite, return or apply the revised copy in the requested form, briefly noting the inferred style and any material uncertainty. For a review, do not modify the source; provide a small number of prioritized observations with replacement wording where useful. When style approval is warranted, show the compact proposal and sample without padding it into a generic style lecture.
